The first three

Three designs. Fifty scarves.

All three are built on the same structure: a central field, four corner cartouches, and a border heavy enough to hold them. It is the layout of a 16th century Persian carpet, and the reason it has lasted five hundred years is that it still reads when the thing is folded into a triangle and knotted at the neck. Reserve whichever one you want. The design with the most reservations on 30 September is the one that gets produced.

No. 01

Peacock Night

Lajward, lapis ground

Peacock feathers drawn open across a lapis field, with cream cartouches at the four corners and small flowers scattered through the fall of the barbs. It reads almost black across a room, then opens up entirely when you are close to it. The peacock has been India's emblem on French silk since at least 1985, when Hermès put one on their own India carré.

Saffron Crocus

Majith, madder ground

Crocus flowers turning around a small gold bowl of saffron threads, on a madder red border the colour of the ground the French called fond rouge when they were still copying it from Gujarat. No. 03

Champa Dusk

Nilak, ink ground

Champa flowers in cream and gold on an ink ground so dark it takes a moment to find the edges. This is the quiet one, and the one that will go with the most in your wardrobe. Mughal court painters built their flowers as composites, half observed and half invented, working from European botanical prints. This does the same thing.

Where the designs come from

The route home

Indian printed cottons reached Marseille in the early 1600s and held their colour in a way European cloth could not. France banned them in 1686 to protect its own industry and broke the printing blocks three years later. The ban lifted in 1759, and Oberkampf founded his manufactory at Jouy-en-Josas the following year. Toile de Jouy exists because the ban on Indian cotton ended. The small floral repeat the world now calls Provençal is an Indian print that changed its name.

These are not borrowing from France. They are taking the same road back.

The design is Indian and Persian. The silk is printed in Hangzhou, which has made it for four thousand years — the one place with a longer claim to the cloth than Lyon has to the print. Neither is pretending to be French, and that is the entire point.

What these are and are not. Original designs, developed digitally and printed on silk. Not block printed, not tie dyed, not embroidered. No artisan cluster made them and I do not use any protected craft name to sell them. If you want the handmade article, buy it from the people whose it is.
