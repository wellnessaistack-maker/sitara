The first three

Three designs. You pick the one I make.

I grew up around Indian textiles, and I could never find a silk scarf that carried that without turning it into a costume. So I drew three. They are built on the same bones, a centre, four corners, and a border heavy enough to hold them, which is the old Persian carpet layout, and the reason it still looks right when the scarf is folded into a triangle and knotted at your neck. Reserve the one you love. Whichever gets the most reservations by 30 September is the one I make first.

No. 01

Peacock Night

Lajward, lapis ground

Peacock feathers drawn open across a lapis field, with cream cartouches at the four corners and small flowers scattered through the fall of the barbs. It reads almost black across a room, then opens up entirely when you are close to it. The peacock has been India's emblem on French silk since at least 1985, when Hermès put one on their own India carré.

No. 02

Saffron Crocus

Majith, madder ground

Crocus flowers turning around a small gold bowl of saffron threads, on a madder red border the colour the French called fond rouge when they were still copying it from Gujarat. This is the warm one. It is also the only design here with a subject rather than a pattern, which is how a scarf earns a second look.

No. 03

Champa Dusk

Nilak, ink ground

Champa flowers in cream and gold on an ink ground so dark it takes a moment to find the edges. This is the quiet one, and the one that will go with the most in your wardrobe. Mughal court painters built their flowers as composites, half observed and half invented, working from European botanical prints. This does the same thing.

Where the designs come from

The short version

Here is something I love. That little floral print everyone calls "Provençal", the very French one, actually started as an Indian print. Indian cotton reached France in the 1600s, France banned it to protect its own mills, and then quietly copied it. My designs skip the French detour and go straight back to the Persian and Mughal work it all came from.

These are original designs, printed on silk. They are not block-printed or embroidered, and I do not borrow any craft's name to sell them. If it is the handmade, hand-blocked thing you want, please buy that from the artisans who make it. This is its own thing, and I would rather be honest about that.
